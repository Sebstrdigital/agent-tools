# Outcome-driven delivery

A clear request to execute an understood task is authority for its ordinary reversible development. Do not require the user to select a mode and then approve a separate Agreement. Advice, a draft proposal or vague readiness is not execution authority. Resolve consequential gaps once; preserve explicit limits and exclusions.

Use a concise task: purpose, intended behavior, acceptance, relevant rules and boundaries. Plan the [manual acceptance handoff](../../docs/workflows/validated-completion.md#manual-acceptance-handoff) from project `AGENTS.md` and settled context; ask only about consequential gaps and keep trivial work proportionate. Conversation, an issue or existing JSON can carry it; no mandatory record or snapshot suite. Templates are optional. No mandatory reading manifests or read receipts. Read relevant source on demand; excerpts must not omit governing constraints. Keep logs on disk. Reuse unchanged context/evidence.

### Early delivery preflight

Before substantial execution, trace the agreed outcome and acceptance through checks, review, handoff and any requested release; keep scope and non-goals visible in the existing task context. Distinguish authority already granted from genuinely unresolved permissions and explicit exclusions. Surface known independent consequential decisions together when useful, including grouped questions where supported; ask dependent decisions later when their prerequisites are settled. Do not seek blanket permission, re-ask settled grants or treat a plan as authority.

If delegating, check that available worker roles and grants can execute the required validation and any authorized release route; availability is not permission. Identify validation side effects and the effective isolation boundary before running checks. Choose the simplest sufficient safe route that preserves required checks and normal user-path acceptance; reuse suitable existing resources rather than create infrastructure by default. If a necessary capability or permission is missing, report the specific gate and continue independent authorized work where safe, without substituting an unapproved release or weakening acceptance.

Direct remains appropriate for a small task: the lead owns implementation, diagnosis, repair and validation. Once delegation is selected, the lead coordinates/scopes work; reads evidence; inspects source diffs, logs, screenshots and release metadata; maintains coordination notes; and alone accepts. Workers execute product and supporting-file edits, diagnosis/repair, tests/build/browser/API validation and environment lifecycle under their fixed role grants and applicable authority. Commit, push, merge-request creation, merge, deploy and publication likewise belong to workers only when applicable authority permits them. Lead shell/API use in delegated work is read-only evidence inspection, never executing tests or other validation. No quick-fix or one-line exception applies. Red checks and independent findings are not lead-takeover exceptions: reconcile worker ownership/resources, then continue, redirect or replace the worker. Switch delegated work to Direct only explicitly after reconciliation, never as a red-check workaround. Ordinary compiler/test/harness corrections are implementation, not repair tokens. Do not invent repair quotas, per-role budgets or cumulative accounting ceremonies. User-agreed limits remain binding, including old narrow packets. Diagnose repeated defects and change strategy; stop for demonstrated no progress, exhausted explicit limits or consequential new authority.

Before marking any milestone complete or claiming readiness, validate its acceptance condition and inspect the evidence for the actual candidate/environment. Follow [validated milestone completion](../../docs/workflows/validated-completion.md), including the normal user path for review environments; setup alone or diagnostic bypasses do not prove readiness. Failed or blocked validation stays unfinished with a reason. On failure, retain evidence and reconcile any partial side effects and resource ownership safely before retrying; do not blindly replay actions. Diagnose, repair in scope and rerun affected acceptance checks under existing authority rather than hand off a failed outcome prematurely. Stop and surface genuine safety, permission or no-progress blockers. Workers execute delegated checks; the lead inspects and accepts, never just forwards a completion claim.

File lists are pointers unless explicitly restricted. Include the minimum supporting files needed for the approved outcome; do not add features or cross exclusions. A materially new behavior, trust boundary, private-data access or required-check change needs a decision, not every new filename.

Plan foreseeable important cases proportionately. The agreed task is the boundary through build, verification and review; the wider product or sprint plan is context, not permission to start its other tasks. During implementation, distinguish defects blocking the agreed outcome from separable discoveries: finish the agreed work and record follow-ups when safe, rather than silently expanding requirements. Classify review findings against the task and safety; do not turn suggestions into requirements. Surface serious risks honestly, distinguishing task blockers from release risks that can be documented separately. If safety or acceptance is blocked, surface it and do not claim completion. For a consequential question, state what was found and its effect on the original task, offer the bounded route where viable, and clearly label options adding requirements or acceptance criteria **Scope expansion**, including material implications. Recommend the bounded route when it can safely meet the agreed outcome; if recommending expansion, explain why the bounded route is inadequate and what additional work it entails. Revisit earlier recommendations when their scope or cost changes; ordinary in-scope repairs need no new approval.

Keep task artifacts and worktrees inside the owning project root. Follow [project-local artifacts and housekeeping](../../docs/workflows/project-artifacts.md) when planning outputs and completion: choose one task workspace, pass paths/cleanup responsibility to workers, retain useful evidence and reconcile disposable assets under applicable authority. External scratch needs an explicit exception; cleanup blockers stay visible.

Read references/delegation.md before delegating. Choose useful roles and context lifetimes; no mandatory fresh builder/validator/reviewer cycle per slice. Independent checks/review should fit the risk and any requested gates. One final integrated check/review can cover related changes; renew affected evidence after repair, not assurance-only full reruns.

The tmux adapter permits concurrent workers in distinct linked worktrees. Same-workspace ownership remains exclusive, including pause and question waits, except for pinned inspection-only reviewers of the same verified clean Git candidate. This check is not a filesystem lock or immutable snapshot; see [parallel guidance](../addy-engineering/references/parallel-work.md). Do not run parallel writers in one checkout. Workers do not nest. Reconcile partial changes, pins and resources before continuation or bounded replacement; never blindly replay side effects or reset explicit limits.

Do not control unrelated applications or stop the app under development without authority. Isolate live-side-effect tests and verify effective test-host isolation before sensitive checks. New private-data access, destructive actions and publication need applicable authority. Finish with the exact useful candidate, actual/reused checks, a consolidated manual checklist and honest gaps. The lead alone accepts; a worker result or green test is not product acceptance or permission to publish.
